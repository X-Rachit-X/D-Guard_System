\begin{abstract}
Multimodal skin-lesion classifiers fail less often for lack of accuracy than for
misplaced confidence: a blurred photograph, missing history, or an image and history
that disagree still yield a confident label. We present \dguard{}, an agentic decision
system that combines a team of diverse specialists with a calibrated, cost-aware choice
among four outcomes: answer with high confidence, request a dermoscopic image, answer
with explicitly low confidence pending clinician confirmation, or defer when the
evidence contradicts itself. Five specialists---evidential heads for the clinical
photograph, dermoscopy and metadata, an externally trained legacy image model, and a
case-based nearest-neighbour vote---are profiled by a \emph{Devil's Advocate} evidence
manager (quality, learned reliability, uncertainty, consistency, missingness and
Mahalanobis out-of-distribution distance) and combined by an EM-style \emph{iterative
diagnosis-adaptive fusion} whose diagnosis-conditioned priors are measured rather than
hand-set. Dempster--Shafer ignorance and conflict, a Mondrian conformal set over
\{benign, malignant\}, a Learn-then-Test threshold, image-quality and
out-of-distribution gates, and a learned acquisition-value estimator drive the decision.
On MILK10k, where every lesion pairs a clinical and a dermoscopic image, adaptive
specialist fusion raises 11-class macro-F1 from 0.376 to 0.453 over evidential fusion
alone. Of 787 held-out patients, 89.6\% receive an answer; high-confidence answers cover
60.2\% of patients at 76.2\% accuracy and miss 3.0\% of malignancies, against 9.3\% for
confidence-based abstention at identical coverage. Under controlled degradation,
evidential ignorance alone grows \emph{more} confident on blurred photographs; a quality
gate removes those answers, while occlusion remains a documented failure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Deep classifiers reach dermatologist-level accuracy on curated images \cite{esteva2017},
yet deployment exposes two structural weaknesses. First, softmax outputs must sum to
one, so a model cannot express that \emph{no} class is supported by the evidence.
Second, concatenation-based multimodal fusion gives every modality a fixed slot, so an
uninformative input adds noise on every case. In an earlier prototype on PAD-UFES-20
\cite{pacheco2020padufes} we observed the consequence directly: adding a weak text
modality to image and metadata \emph{lowered} macro-F1 from 0.653 to 0.633.

A clinician facing the same uncertainty does not guess: they consult colleagues, look
more closely with a dermatoscope, or refer the patient. The first version of \dguard{}
imitated the consultation---a panel of specialist models, an evidence auditor, and an
iterative fusion that re-weights sources once a working diagnosis emerges---but its
weights and thresholds were set by hand. A second design added the missing statistical
machinery---calibrated deferral, risk control and a cost-aware decision to acquire
dermoscopy---but used a single model. This paper unifies the two in one trained,
evaluated system.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{specialist team} combining in-domain evidential heads, an external
legacy model and a case-based specialist, profiled by a \textbf{Devil's Advocate}
evidence manager whose reliabilities are \emph{learned} and whose out-of-distribution
detector is calibrated (Secs.~\ref{sec:team}--\ref{sec:evidence}).
\item \textbf{Iterative diagnosis-adaptive fusion} with \emph{measured}
diagnosis-conditioned priors, improving 11-class macro-F1 by 0.077 over evidential
fusion (Sec.~\ref{sec:fusion}, Table~\ref{tab:fusion}).
\item A \textbf{calibrated four-outcome policy}---high-confidence answer, acquisition,
low-confidence answer, deferral---combining Dempster--Shafer conflict, a
benign/malignant conformal set, a Learn-then-Test bound, quality and
out-of-distribution gates, and an explicit cost matrix (Sec.~\ref{sec:policy}).
\item A \textbf{measured value of dermoscopy} on paired data, with a learned
acquisition estimator (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).
\item An \textbf{agent pipeline and care platform}: quality agent, retrieval of similar
confirmed cases, a report writer whose claims are verified against the evidence, a
prioritised clinician queue, and follow-up with change detection (Sec.~\ref{sec:system}).
\item \textbf{DERMA-STRESS} on the trained system, exposing a failure of evidential
ignorance under blur, its remedy, and a remaining failure under occlusion
(Sec.~\ref{sec:stress}).
\end{enumerate}

The individual techniques are established; the contribution is their integration into
one calibrated decision and its measurement on paired clinical--dermoscopic data.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Multimodal dermatology.} MetaBlock \cite{pacheco2021metablock} modulates image
features with metadata; Derm7pt \cite{kawahara2019derm7pt} pairs clinical and
dermoscopic images for the seven-point checklist; PanDerm \cite{yan2025panderm} is a
dermatology foundation model. Multi-task systems such as HOT \cite{mehta2025hotai} can
recommend dermoscopy. These works improve prediction; they do not decide \emph{whether}
a prediction should be issued.

\textbf{Uncertainty and evidential fusion.} Evidential deep learning \cite{sensoy2018edl}
places a Dirichlet over class probabilities, giving a per-sample ignorance mass. Trusted
Multi-View Classification \cite{han2021tmc} combines per-view opinions with
Dempster--Shafer's rule \cite{dempster1967,shafer1976} in subjective-logic form
\cite{josang2016}. Temperature scaling \cite{guo2017calibration} and isotonic regression
\cite{zadrozny2002isotonic} calibrate confidences; Mahalanobis scores
\cite{lee2018mahalanobis} detect out-of-distribution inputs.

\textbf{Conformal prediction and risk control.} Split conformal prediction
\cite{vovk2005,angelopoulos2021gentle} yields label sets with distribution-free coverage;
APS \cite{romano2020aps} adapts to difficulty, LAC sets \cite{sadinle2019lac} are
smallest on average, and class-conditional calibration protects subgroups
\cite{lu2022fair}. Learn-then-Test \cite{angelopoulos2025ltt} and conformal risk control
\cite{angelopoulos2022crc} certify thresholds for general risks.

\textbf{Selective prediction and deferral.} Chow's rule \cite{chow1970}, selective
classification \cite{geifman2017selective} and learning to defer
\cite{mozannar2020defer} abstain or hand off; Traub et al.\ \cite{traub2024augrc}
introduce AUGRC for evaluating such systems.

\textbf{Retrieval.} Retrieval-augmented generation \cite{lewis2020rag} and
nearest-neighbour classification \cite{cover1967knn} supply case-based evidence; we use
both, but keep retrieved documents out of the calibrated decision.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:problem}

A case $x$ comprises a clinical photograph $x_c$, an optional dermoscopic image $x_d$ and
metadata $x_m$, with label $y\in\{1,\dots,K\}$, $K=11$, and malignant set $\mathcal{M}$
($|\mathcal{M}|=5$). The system outputs \PROCEED{}$(\hat y,\text{tier})$,
\ACQUIRE{}(dermoscopy) or \DEFER{}, always with a tentative opinion. Losses follow a
declared matrix $L_{y\hat y}$: 0 if correct, 1 within the same group (benign or
malignant), $c_{\text{miss}}$ for a malignant lesion called benign, and $c_{\text{fa}}=2$
for the reverse; acquiring dermoscopy costs $c_A=0.5$ and clinician review $c_D=1.5$. We
report $c_{\text{miss}}\in\{2,3,5,10\}$ and use 5 as the operating point.

\subsection{Specialist team}\label{sec:team}

\textbf{S1--S3: evidential heads.} A ResNet50 \cite{he2016resnet} shared by both image
types and a residual metadata MLP feed three heads producing evidence
$e_m=\mathrm{softplus}(f_m(z_m))$. With $\alpha_m=e_m+1$ and $S_m=\sum_k\alpha_{m,k}$,
belief and ignorance are
\begin{equation}
b_{m,k}=e_{m,k}/S_m,\qquad u_m=K/S_m,
\end{equation}
trained with the evidential loss of \cite{sensoy2018edl}. Each head contributes its
pignistic distribution $p_m=b_m+u_m/K$.

\textbf{S4: legacy external model.} The image specialist of the original
\dguard{}---ResNet50 features, channel attention and a two-layer self-attention
encoder---trained on PAD-UFES-20 smartphone photographs, is applied unchanged to MILK10k
clinical photographs. Its six classes map onto six of the eleven MILK10k classes; the
other five receive a small uniform floor so that an unsupported class is ``unknown''
rather than ``impossible''.

\textbf{S5: case-based specialist.} The 15 most similar \emph{training} lesions, by cosine
similarity of the trained model's embeddings \cite{cover1967knn}, vote with weights
$\exp(5\,\mathrm{sim})$ and Laplace smoothing.

\subsection{Devil's Advocate: evidence profile}\label{sec:evidence}

For each specialist $m$ and case $x$ the evidence manager computes:
\textbf{quality} $Q_m$, the share of four image statistics (sharpness as the variance of
the Laplacian, brightness, contrast, glare) inside the 5th--95th percentile of training
photographs, 1 for metadata, and mean neighbour similarity for S5;
\textbf{uncertainty} $U_m$, the normalised entropy of $p_m$;
\textbf{reliability} $R_m$, a logistic regression fitted on validation lesions that
predicts whether specialist $m$ is correct from $[Q_m,U_m,\max_k p_{m,k},A_m]$, where
$A_m$ is the share of other specialists agreeing with $m$'s top class---replacing the
untrained network of the original design;
\textbf{consistency} $C=1-\binom{|M|}{2}^{-1}\sum_{i<j}\mathrm{JS}(p_i\Vert p_j)/\log 2$
\cite{lin1991js};
\textbf{missingness}, the share of absent specialists; and
\textbf{OOD}, the Mahalanobis distance \cite{lee2018mahalanobis} of the case embedding to
the nearest class mean under a Ledoit--Wolf shared covariance \cite{ledoit2004}, flagged
above the validation 99th percentile.

\subsection{Iterative diagnosis-adaptive fusion}\label{sec:fusion}

How much to trust each source depends on the suspected diagnosis---dermoscopy matters
more for squamous lesions than for basal cell carcinoma---yet the diagnosis is unknown
until the sources are fused. The original design resolved this with an EM-style
\cite{dempster1977em} alternation; we keep the algorithm and replace its hand-typed prior
with a measured one, $\pi_m(c)=\Pr(\text{specialist } m\text{ correct}\mid y=c)$,
estimated on validation with Laplace smoothing (Algorithm~\ref{alg:fusion}). The
coefficients $a,b,g$, the conflict penalty $\rho$ and a temperature $T$
\cite{guo2017calibration} are chosen on validation (grid search for macro-F1, then $T$ by
negative log-likelihood). The displayed confidence is the isotonic
\cite{zadrozny2002isotonic} estimate of $\Pr(\hat y=y\mid\max_k p_k)$ fitted on validation,
so it reads as the probability that the stated diagnosis is correct.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Iterative diagnosis-adaptive fusion}
\label{alg:fusion}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE specialist distributions $\{p_m\}$, $Q_m R_m$, presence mask $\mu_m$
\STATE $w_m \leftarrow \softmax_m\big(a\log(Q_mR_m)\big)\,\mu_m$; renormalise
\STATE $p \leftarrow \sum_m w_m p_m$; \ $\hat y \leftarrow \argmax_k p_k$
\FOR{$t=1$ \TO $3$}
  \STATE $\pi_m \leftarrow \pi_m(\hat y)$ \COMMENT{measured prior}
  \STATE $\kappa_m \leftarrow 1$ if $\argmax p_m=\hat y$, else $\rho$
  \STATE $w'_m \leftarrow \softmax_m\big(a\log(Q_mR_m)+b\log\pi_m+g\log\kappa_m\big)\,\mu_m$
  \STATE $p' \leftarrow \sum_m w'_m p_m$; \ $\hat y' \leftarrow \argmax p'$
  \IF{$\hat y'=\hat y$ \AND $\max_m|w'_m-w_m|<0.05$}
    \STATE \textbf{break}
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $(w,p,\hat y)\leftarrow(w',p',\hat y')$
\ENDFOR
\RETURN temperature-scaled $p$, weights $w$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

In parallel, the three evidential heads are combined by Dempster--Shafer's rule
\cite{han2021tmc}:
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{DS}}=\textstyle\sum_{i\ne j}b_{1,i}b_{2,j},\quad
b_k=\dfrac{b_{1,k}b_{2,k}+b_{1,k}u_2+b_{2,k}u_1}{1-C_{\mathrm{DS}}},\quad
u=\dfrac{u_1u_2}{1-C_{\mathrm{DS}}}.
\end{equation}
The vacuous opinion $(b{=}0,u{=}1)$ is the rule's identity element, so an absent modality
has exactly no effect, and $C_{\mathrm{DS}}$---carried as the maximum over the
fold---separates \emph{contradiction} from shared \emph{ignorance}. The policy reads the
adaptive-fusion distribution $p$ together with the Dempster--Shafer $u$ and
$C_{\mathrm{DS}}$.

\subsection{Calibrated four-outcome policy}\label{sec:policy}

\textbf{Label.} The stated diagnosis is $\hat y=\argmax_k p_k$ with expected loss
$r_P=\sum_y p_yL_{y\hat y}$. Stating the minimum-cost label instead (classic Chow
\cite{chow1970}) shifts answers toward malignancy and, on this 73.6\%-malignant
population, labelled 98.5\% of lesions malignant.

\textbf{Gates.} (i) \emph{Conflict}: $C_{\mathrm{DS}}$ above its validation 90th
percentile forces \DEFER{}. (ii) \emph{False reassurance}: a Mondrian \cite{lu2022fair}
LAC \cite{sadinle2019lac} conformal set over $\{\text{benign},\text{malignant}\}$
($\alpha=0.1$) blocks a confident benign statement while ``malignant'' remains in the
set. (iii) \emph{Risk}: Learn-then-Test \cite{angelopoulos2025ltt} with
Hoeffding--Bentkus $p$-values selects $\hat\lambda$ on $1-u$ such that
$\Pr(R(\hat\lambda)\le 0.05)\ge 0.9$ for the malignancy-miss rate. (iv) \emph{Quality}:
a photograph beyond the 1st/99th percentile of training statistics cannot receive a
high-confidence answer. (v) \emph{OOD}: likewise for a Mahalanobis outlier.

\textbf{Acquisition.} A gradient-boosted regressor $\hat h$, fitted on validation,
predicts the loss reduction from adding the lesion's dermoscopy given stage-A evidence;
\ACQUIRE{} costs $r_A=r_P-\hat h+c_A$. One acquisition round is allowed.

\textbf{Outcomes.} If no gate fires and answering is cheapest: \PROCEED{} (high). If a
gate fires or $c_D<r_P$: \ACQUIRE{} if $r_A<c_D$ and dermoscopy is still available;
otherwise \PROCEED{} (low)---the diagnosis is stated, labelled unreliable, and queued for
confirmation. Only conflict (or absent evidence) yields \DEFER{}. The high tier coincides
with a standard defer-on-any-gate policy, so every guarantee and baseline comparison
concerns the same answers. Every outcome carries the most probable diagnosis, a
three-class differential, $\Pr(\text{malignant})$ and an urgency flag.

\textbf{Consensus hierarchy.} The original four-level hierarchy is kept as an
interpretable summary: Level~1 (strong consensus) for a high-tier answer with at least
three agreeing specialists, Level~2 for any other high-tier answer, Level~3 for
acquisition or a low-tier answer, and Level~4 for deferral.

\section{Agents and Care Platform}\label{sec:system}

Only the decision engine decides; the agents check, retrieve and write.
The \textbf{quality agent} computes the image statistics above and advises the patient.
The \textbf{librarian} returns the six most similar \emph{training} lesions
\cite{lewis2020rag} with their histopathology-confirmed diagnoses and photographs; its
index refuses to load unless built from the training split, because a held-out
neighbour would leak its label into what a clinician reads.
The \textbf{report agent} writes a structured report, optionally rewritten by a local
LLM and accepted only if every diagnosis named appears in the evidence, every percentage
matches an evidence value within one point, and the confidence tier and confirmation
requirement survive; otherwise the structured report is used.
The \textbf{care platform} handles patient intake, dermoscopy requests, a clinician queue
ordered by outcome and urgency (review, confirm, sign-off), confirm/override with
instructions and follow-ups, follow-up re-assessment with change detection (an increase
of 0.15 in $\Pr(\text{malignant})$ or a new malignant call returns the case as urgent),
and an append-only audit log recording the policy version of every decision. It is
exposed as a web application and a REST API.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Data.} MILK10k \cite{milk10k}: 5{,}240 lesions, each with one clinical close-up and
one dermoscopic image (10{,}480 byte-distinct images), 11 classes, 95.7\%
histopathologically confirmed, 71.7\% malignant. No patient identifiers are published, so
the split is by lesion: train 3{,}667, validation 786, test 787, with both images of a
lesion in the same split.

\textbf{Training.} S1--S3 share one network trained on a Kaggle GPU for 25 epochs
(backbone frozen for 3, modality dropout 0.25, class weights), best epoch by validation
macro-F1. S4 is used as released; S5 uses training embeddings. All fusion, reliability,
calibration, conformal, Learn-then-Test, acquisition and gate parameters are fitted on
validation; the test split is scored once.

\textbf{Protocol.} Stage A uses the photograph and metadata; cases routed to \ACQUIRE{}
are re-assessed in stage B with the lesion's real dermoscopic image.

\textbf{Baselines.} B1 always answer (stage A); B2 always acquire, then answer; B3
max-softmax abstention at matched coverage, without and with dermoscopy for all; B5
acquisition for a random or highest-entropy subset at our acquisition rate. All
baselines use the same fused probabilities.

\textbf{Metrics.} Accuracy, macro-F1 and balanced accuracy; malignant-vs-benign AUROC;
coverage (high-tier share); malignancy-miss rate among answers; mean cost under $L$;
acquisition efficiency $\mathrm{AE}=(G_\pi-G_{\text{rand}})/(G_{\text{oracle}}-G_{\text{rand}})$;
and AUGRC \cite{traub2024augrc}.

raises malignant-vs-benign AUROC from 0.799 to 0.856. The legacy model, trained on a
different dataset and camera type, transfers poorly (21.9\% accuracy); the case-based
specialist is the strongest single source.

\section{Robustness: DERMA-STRESS}\label{sec:stress}

We degrade the stage-A input of 300 test lesions without retraining
(Table~\ref{tab:stress}). Gaussian blur \emph{lowers} mean ignorance (0.679 to 0.605) and
\emph{raises} the high-confidence rate (49\% to 53\%), and on under-exposed photographs
the high-confidence answers miss 13.8\% of malignancies: evidential ignorance alone does
not detect these failures. The quality agent flags every blurred and every under-exposed
photograph, and with the quality gate neither produces a high-confidence answer. Noise,
JPEG compression and withheld metadata raise ignorance and sharply increase dermoscopy
requests (28\% clean; 51\%, 42\% and 42\%). Two failures remain: occluded lesions keep
14\% high-confidence answers that miss 9.3\% of malignancies, and with metadata withheld
the miss rate rises from 4.3\% to 6.4\%. Neither the evidential signals nor the image
statistics fully register these, which motivates explicit occlusion and
missing-information checks.

\section{Discussion and Negative Results}\label{sec:negative}

\textbf{What the original design got right.} Re-weighting sources per case by evidence
and by the suspected diagnosis is worth 0.077 macro-F1 once the weights are learned
rather than hand-set, and the learned reliability is what makes a mixed team safe: it
discounts the weak external specialist automatically.

\textbf{Full fusion of the heads does not beat the best pair.} Within the evidential
network, all three inputs score 0.379 macro-F1 against 0.403 for dermoscopy plus
metadata; team fusion recovers and exceeds this, but the head-level hypothesis is not
supported.

\textbf{Legacy model.} The original PAD-UFES-20 image model reaches only 21.9\% accuracy
on MILK10k; cross-dataset transfer of a smartphone-photo model is poor.

\textbf{Post-hoc imbalance correction did not transfer.} Logit adjustment tuned on
validation raised validation macro-F1 from 0.361 to 0.439 but lowered test macro-F1 from
0.376 to 0.359.

\textbf{Ignorance misses blur; nothing yet catches occlusion} (Sec.~\ref{sec:stress}).

\textbf{Mean cost favours always answering.} Every case needing a clinician is charged
1.5; the value of \dguard{} lies in the safety of its high-confidence answers at matched
coverage, not in throughput.

\textbf{Rare classes.} Three classes with 1--6 test lesions are never predicted.

% =============================================================================
\section{Limitations}

Results come from one training seed and an ImageNet ResNet50; further seeds and a
dermatology foundation encoder \cite{yan2025panderm} remain to be run. The validation
split selects the epoch and fits every fusion and decision parameter. MILK10k lacks
patient and centre identifiers, so splits are lesion-level and centre shift cannot be
evaluated; it is a referral population (73.6\% of test lesions malignant), so no
screening claim is made. The guarantees assume exchangeability and do not hold under
distribution shift; the quality and OOD gates are heuristics on top. The cost matrix is
declared, not derived. The system is a research prototype without clinical validation.

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}

\dguard{} turns a team of imperfect specialists into calibrated clinical actions.
Evidence-aware, diagnosis-adaptive fusion---the core idea of the original system, now
learned from data---improves 11-class macro-F1 by 0.077. Calibrated gates and a
cost-aware policy let the system answer nine in ten patients and give high-confidence
answers to six in ten with a third of the malignancy-miss rate of confidence-based
abstention, ask for dermoscopy when it pays, and defer only when the evidence
contradicts itself.

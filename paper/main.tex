\begin{abstract}
Skin-lesion classifiers return a confident label even when the evidence does not
support one: a softmax output cannot express ignorance, and naive multimodal fusion
cannot discount an uninformative input. We present \dguard{}, a decision layer that,
for each patient, chooses among three actions---answer (\PROCEED{}), request a
dermoscopic image (\ACQUIRE{}), or hand the case to a clinician (\DEFER{}). Each input
modality is given its own evidential head producing belief and ignorance; opinions are
combined with Dempster--Shafer's rule, which removes absent modalities exactly and
separates \emph{conflict} between sources from shared \emph{ignorance}. A
class-conditional conformal set over \{benign, malignant\} blocks false reassurance, a
Learn-then-Test threshold certifies the malignancy-miss risk, and an explicit cost
matrix with a learned acquisition-value estimator selects the cheapest action.
Deferred cases still receive an unconfirmed tentative opinion and an urgency flag. On
MILK10k, where every lesion has a paired clinical and dermoscopic image, the
acquisition question is measured rather than simulated. On 787 held-out lesions,
\dguard{} answers 36.8\% of cases with a 1.7\% malignancy-miss rate, against 5.2\% for
confidence-based abstention at identical coverage, while requesting dermoscopy for
6.4\% of patients. We report the negative results alongside: fusing all modalities
does not beat the best pair, and on mean cost an always-answer policy is cheaper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Deep classifiers can match dermatologists on curated images~\cite{esteva2017}, yet
deployment fails less often for lack of accuracy than for misplaced confidence: a blurred
photograph, a missing history, or an image and history that point to different
diagnoses all still produce a confident label. Two properties of standard pipelines cause
this. First, softmax probabilities must sum to one, so the model has no way to say that
\emph{no} class is supported. Second, concatenation-based multimodal fusion gives every
modality a fixed slot, so an uninformative modality contributes noise on every case. On
PAD-UFES-20~\cite{pacheco2020padufes} we measured the consequence directly: adding a
weak text modality to image and metadata \emph{lowered} macro-F1 from 0.653 to 0.633.

A clinician facing the same uncertainty does not simply guess. They either look more
closely---for skin lesions, typically with a dermatoscope---or refer the patient. We
formalise this as a three-action decision:
\begin{itemize}
\item \PROCEED{}: state a diagnosis now;
\item \ACQUIRE{}: request a dermoscopic image, then decide again;
\item \DEFER{}: route to a clinician, with the system's tentative opinion attached.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A decision rule that integrates per-modality evidential opinions, Dempster--Shafer
fusion, a benign/malignant conformal gate, Learn-then-Test risk control and an explicit
cost matrix into a single calibrated \PROCEED{}/\ACQUIRE{}/\DEFER{} policy
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).
\item A measurement of the value of acquiring dermoscopy on real paired data
(MILK10k~\cite{milk10k}), with a learned acquisition-value estimator evaluated against
random and uncertainty-based acquisition (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).
\item A working care platform---patient intake, a prioritised clinician queue, follow-up
with change detection, and a full audit trail---built around the policy
(Sec.~\ref{sec:system}).
\item A transparent report of what did not work, including a fusion hypothesis that
failed and two design errors uncovered by the evaluation (Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}).
\end{enumerate}

None of the individual components is new; the contribution is their integration into a
calibrated decision and the measurement of that decision on paired data.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Uncertainty and evidential fusion.} Evidential deep learning~\cite{sensoy2018edl}
places a Dirichlet distribution over class probabilities, giving a per-sample ignorance
mass. Trusted Multi-View Classification~\cite{han2021tmc} combines per-view Dirichlet
opinions with Dempster--Shafer combination~\cite{dempster1967,shafer1976} in the
subjective-logic formulation~\cite{josang2016}. We adopt this fusion and add a decision
layer on top.

\textbf{Conformal prediction and risk control.} Split conformal
prediction~\cite{vovk2005,angelopoulos2021gentle} produces prediction sets with
distribution-free coverage. APS~\cite{romano2020aps} adapts set size to difficulty; LAC
sets~\cite{sadinle2019lac} are smallest on average. Class-conditional (Mondrian)
calibration gives per-class coverage, relevant when groups differ~\cite{lu2022fair}.
Learn-then-Test~\cite{angelopoulos2025ltt} and conformal risk
control~\cite{angelopoulos2022crc} calibrate thresholds for general risks with
finite-sample guarantees.

\textbf{Selective prediction and deferral.} Chow's rule~\cite{chow1970} rejects when
expected cost exceeds a reject cost; selective classification~\cite{geifman2017selective}
and learning to defer~\cite{mozannar2020defer} extend this to deep models. Traub et
al.~\cite{traub2024augrc} show that AURC is unsuitable for aggregating across thresholds
and propose AUGRC, which we use as the ranking metric.

\textbf{Multimodal dermatology.} MetaBlock~\cite{pacheco2021metablock} fuses metadata into
image features; Derm7pt~\cite{kawahara2019derm7pt} pairs clinical and dermoscopic images
for the seven-point checklist. PanDerm~\cite{yan2025panderm} is a dermatology foundation
model. HOT AI~\cite{mehta2025hotai} recommends dermoscopy in a multi-task setting; our
work differs in calibrating the acquire/defer decision with explicit risk control and in
measuring acquisition value on paired data.

\subsection{Evidential opinions per modality}
For $K$ classes, a head $f_m$ maps the embedding of modality $m$ to non-negative
evidence $e_m=\mathrm{softplus}(f_m(z_m))\in\mathbb{R}^K_{\ge 0}$. With
$\alpha_m=e_m+1$ and $S_m=\sum_k\alpha_{m,k}$,
\begin{equation}
b_{m,k}=\frac{e_{m,k}}{S_m},\qquad u_m=\frac{K}{S_m},\qquad \sum_k b_{m,k}+u_m=1.
\end{equation}
The ignorance $u_m$ approaches one when the head finds no evidence. Heads are trained
with the evidential loss of~\cite{sensoy2018edl} and an annealed KL term. An absent
modality is assigned the vacuous opinion $(b=\mathbf{0},u=1)$.

\subsection{Dempster--Shafer fusion}
Two opinions are combined as
\begin{align}
C &= \textstyle\sum_{i\ne j} b_{1,i}\,b_{2,j}, \\
b_k &= \frac{b_{1,k}b_{2,k}+b_{1,k}u_2+b_{2,k}u_1}{1-C},\qquad
u=\frac{u_1u_2}{1-C}.
\end{align}
The rule is commutative and associative, so modalities are folded in any order. The
vacuous opinion is its identity element: an absent modality changes the result by
exactly zero, without imputation. We carry the fused conflict as the maximum $C$ over all
pairwise steps, so that a strongly conflicting pair is not diluted by later agreement.
The policy reads the pignistic probability $p_k=b_k+u/K$.

Two properties matter for the decision. First, a modality with high $u_m$ has little
influence on the fused belief, so per-case \emph{dynamic} modality weights emerge; we
report them as $w_m=(1-u_m)/\sum_{m'}(1-u_{m'})$. Second, $C$ and $u$ are distinct: two
confident sources that disagree produce high $C$ and low $u$ (a human should arbitrate),
while two ignorant sources produce low $C$ and high $u$ (more evidence may help).
Distribution-divergence measures report the second case as agreement.

\subsection{Benign/malignant conformal gate}
The safety-relevant question is whether malignancy can be ruled out, so the gate uses a
two-outcome set over $\{\text{benign},\text{malignant}\}$ with
$p_{\text{mal}}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}p_k$. We use the LAC score $s=1-p_{y}$~\cite{sadinle2019lac}
with a separate quantile per outcome (Mondrian):
\begin{equation}
\hat q_c=\mathrm{Quantile}\!\left(\{s_i: y_i=c\};\ \tfrac{\lceil (n_c+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil}{n_c}\right),
\end{equation}
so that at least $1-\alpha$ of malignant lesions retain ``malignant'' in their set. The gate
blocks \PROCEED{} when the stated label is benign while ``malignant'' is in the set
(false reassurance), or when the set is empty.

\subsection{Risk control with Learn-then-Test}
For a confidence score $g=1-u$ and threshold $\lambda$, let $R(\lambda)$ be the
malignancy-miss rate among accepted cases. Learn-then-Test~\cite{angelopoulos2025ltt}
tests $H_\lambda: R(\lambda)>\alpha$ over a grid with Hoeffding--Bentkus $p$-values and
fixed-sequence testing, returning $\hat\lambda$ such that
\begin{equation}
\Pr\big(R(\hat\lambda)\le\alpha\big)\ge 1-\delta
\end{equation}
over the calibration draw, under exchangeability. If no $\lambda$ is certified the
procedure reports so rather than returning a threshold.

\subsection{Cost-aware three-action policy}
Let $L\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}$ be the loss of stating $\hat y$ when the truth is $y$:
0 if correct, 1 if both are in the same group, $c_{\text{miss}}$ for a malignant lesion
called benign, and $c_{\text{fa}}=2$ for the reverse. The stated label is the most probable
class $\hat y=\arg\max_k p_k$, with expected loss $r_P=\sum_y p_y L_{y,\hat y}$. With
deferral cost $c_D=1.5$, acquisition cost $c_A=0.5$ and an estimate $\hat h(x)$ of the
loss reduction dermoscopy would bring,
\begin{equation}
r_A=r_P-\hat h(x)+c_A,\qquad r_D=c_D,
\end{equation}
and the policy takes the cheapest admissible action. Conflict above the validation 90th
percentile forces \DEFER{} (more evidence does not resolve a contradiction); gate
violations remove \PROCEED{}. At most one acquisition round is allowed. The estimator
$\hat h$ is a gradient-boosted regressor fitted on validation lesions, mapping
stage-A evidence ($u$, $C$, $\max_k p_k$, $p_{\text{mal}}$, normalised entropy, $u_m$) to
the realised loss reduction from adding dermoscopy.

\textbf{Tentative opinion.} Every decision---including \DEFER{} and \ACQUIRE{}---carries
the most probable diagnosis, a three-class differential, $p_{\text{mal}}$, and an urgency
flag (urgent if $p_{\text{mal}}\ge 0.3$ or a malignant class is in the conformal set). It is
labelled unconfirmed and is never emitted as a diagnosis; a deferred patient is told what
the system suspects and that a clinician must confirm it.

\section{System}\label{sec:system}

The policy is embedded in a care platform with three roles. \emph{Patients} open a case,
upload a photograph and answer intake questions; the system assesses and either returns an
assessment with instructions (\PROCEED{}), asks for a dermoscopic image and re-assesses
(\ACQUIRE{}), or places the case in the clinician queue (\DEFER{}). \emph{Clinicians} see a
queue ordered by deferral, then urgency, then age, with images, the tentative opinion,
$u$, $C$, per-modality votes and dynamic weights, and the measured reliability of each
modality for the tentative diagnosis; they confirm, override, or request an in-person
visit, write an instruction and schedule a follow-up. At follow-up a new photograph is
re-assessed and compared with the previous assessment; an increase of 0.15 in
$p_{\text{mal}}$ or a change to a malignant tentative diagnosis returns the case to the
queue as urgent. Every event is written to an append-only audit log together with the
policy configuration that produced it. An agent pipeline (specialist models, an evidence
auditor, a retrieval agent and a report writer with claim verification) produces the
narrative; retrieval of similar training cases is restricted to the training split and
excluded from the decision path, because exchangeability-based guarantees do not survive
a decision that depends on a changing corpus.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Data.} MILK10k~\cite{milk10k}: 5{,}240 lesions, each with one clinical close-up
and one dermoscopic image (10{,}480 images, all byte-distinct), 11 diagnostic classes,
95.7\% histopathologically confirmed. The population is referral-like: 71.7\% of lesions are
malignant and basal cell carcinoma alone accounts for 48.1\%. No patient identifiers are
published, so splits are by lesion (train 3{,}667 / validation 786 / test 787); both images
of a lesion always fall in the same split. Malignant classes are AKIEC, BCC, MAL\_OTH,
MEL and SCCKA.

\textbf{Model.} A ResNet50~\cite{he2016resnet} backbone pretrained on ImageNet is shared
by both image types, with a 256-d projection and a separate evidential head per image
type; metadata (age, sex, site, skin tone, seven MONET concept scores; 26 features) uses a
residual MLP and its own head. Training: 25 epochs, backbone frozen for 3, modality
dropout 0.25, best epoch by validation macro-F1 (epoch 23). One seed (42).

\textbf{Protocol.} Stage A uses the clinical photograph and metadata. Cases routed to
\ACQUIRE{} are re-assessed in stage B with the lesion's real dermoscopic image. Conformal
sets ($\alpha=0.10$), conflict thresholds, LTT ($\alpha=0.05$, $\delta=0.10$) and $\hat h$
are fitted on validation; test is scored once.

\textbf{Baselines.} B1: always answer from stage A. B2: always acquire dermoscopy and
answer. B3: max-softmax abstention at matched coverage, with and without dermoscopy for
all. B5: acquire dermoscopy for a random subset, or for the highest-entropy subset, at
\dguard{}'s acquisition rate, then answer.

\textbf{Metrics.} Coverage (fraction answered); accuracy and balanced accuracy among
answered; malignancy-miss rate among answered (malignant called benign); false-alarm rate
among answered benign lesions; mean cost per case under $L$; acquisition efficiency
$\mathrm{AE}=(G_\pi-G_{\text{rand}})/(G_{\text{oracle}}-G_{\text{rand}})$; AUGRC~\cite{traub2024augrc}.

\subsection{Diagnosis-dependent modality reliability}

On validation, the fraction of lesions for which each modality alone names the true class
differs by diagnosis: for SCC/keratoacanthoma dermoscopy is far more reliable than the
clinical photograph (0.74 vs.\ 0.36), for benign keratoses likewise (0.31 vs.\ 0.17), while
for melanoma the clinical photograph is the most reliable single source (0.52 vs.\ 0.40).
These measured values replace a hand-specified prior used in an earlier version of the
system, which assumed 0.96 image reliability for melanoma. They are shown to clinicians
and never alter a probability.

\section{What Did Not Work}\label{sec:negative}

\textbf{Full fusion does not beat the best pair} (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}); the fusion
hypothesis is not supported by this run.

\textbf{Cost-optimal labelling degenerates.} Stating the label of minimum expected cost
(Chow's rule with $c_{\text{miss}}=10$) called 98.5\% of lesions malignant, giving a zero
miss rate for a trivial reason. We therefore state the most probable class and let the cost
matrix decide only whether it may be stated.

\textbf{Two-class APS degenerates.} Without randomisation, the APS score of any lesion
whose true outcome is ranked second equals one; the benign quantile collapsed to one and
the gate deferred every case. A symmetric gate that also blocked ``likely malignant''
statements compounded this. LAC scores and a gate restricted to false reassurance fixed
both.

\textbf{A fusion implementation drifted.} The training script's own copy of the fold
reported only the final pairwise conflict, which on MILK10k is always against the absent
text modality and therefore zero. Conflict was not in the loss, so weights were unaffected;
we recompute it from the checkpoint.

\textbf{Mean cost favours always answering} (Table~\ref{tab:policy}). Each deferral costs
1.5 and \dguard{} defers 63\% of cases. The advantage of \dguard{} is the safety of the
answers it gives at matched coverage, not throughput.

\section{Limitations}

Results are from one training seed and an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50; the image encoder
limits coverage, and a dermatology foundation encoder~\cite{yan2025panderm} is the expected
remedy. The validation split selects the epoch and calibrates the policy. MILK10k lacks
patient identifiers, so lesion-level splits leave residual patient-level leakage, and it
publishes no centre identifier, so centre shift cannot be evaluated. The population is a
referral population; no screening claim is made. The guarantees assume exchangeability
and do not hold under distribution shift. The cost matrix is declared, not derived. The
system is a research prototype without clinical validation.

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}

\dguard{} turns per-modality evidence into a calibrated choice among answering, acquiring
dermoscopy and deferring, and keeps deferral informative by attaching a tentative opinion
and urgency to every case. On paired clinical--dermoscopic data it cuts the malignancy-miss
rate by a factor of three relative to confidence-based abstention at equal coverage while
acquiring dermoscopy for few patients. Its main limitation---answering only about a third
of cases alone---is set by the classifier rather than the policy, which makes stronger
encoders and multi-seed evaluation the immediate next steps.
